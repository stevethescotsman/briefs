No hot water this morning — and the smart meter that saw it coming

A cold shower, a schedule that looked fine, and a boiler quietly refusing to fire. The thing that gave it away wasn't a thermostat — it was a gas meter already sitting in the house.

A smart-home owner · Sept 2026 Home Assistant Real data

Tuesday morning. I turn on the hot tap and it runs cold. Not "warm" — properly cold. The tank hasn't been heated.

So I do the obvious thing: I open the heating app and check the schedule. All good. The hot-water window ran. The radiators look scheduled too. Everything says it should be working.

Then I check the actual hot water tank. It's cold. Not just used up — it never got heated at all. So I climb up to check the boiler, and there it is: the pressure's dropped. The boiler won't light on low pressure. It's dead in the attic — which, of course, it would be on the one cold morning of the year.

The fix is manual and fiddly: top up the pressure, then reboot the boiler from the isolator switch in the tank cupboard. It works, and by mid-morning the tank's heating again.

But the question nags at me all day: the schedule said everything was fine, and nothing warned me until I put my hand on a cold tank.

Why the schedule lied

The heart of the problem is that the app and the boiler are moving on two different clocks.

My heating is run by two smart controllers — a Heatmiser Neo for the tank and radiators, and Tado for other zones. Both are excellent at sending a signal to the boiler: "please fire."

But that signal is one-way. When the boiler can't light — low pressure, a lockout, a gas problem — the controllers don't know. They keep showing the schedule as running, because they asked for it. They have no idea nothing happened.

The real issue Command is not proof. "I asked the boiler to fire" and "the boiler fired" are two different facts. No controller setting will ever tell you the whole story — you have to measure the outcome.

The thing already in the house

The fix wasn't new hardware. It was data Home Assistant was already collecting.

My Home Assistant setup already has a smart gas meter wired in (through my energy supplier). Its whole job is to measure gas actually burned — the outcome. It reports in half-hourly chunks.

That's the missing signal. It's now a simple check:

# is the boiler being asked to fire?
any heat demand is ON

# ...but has no gas been burned for an hour?
gas used in the last hour < 0.05 m³

# ...then it isn't lighting. Say so, loudly.
→ notify: "Possible Boiler Issue"
The fix A "boiler demanded but not firing" watchdog, built in Home Assistant. Zero new hardware, zero wiring, zero cost. It reads data the house already had and raises the alarm the moment a dead window closes.

And the data proves it wasn't one-off

Pulling the week's gas history showed the failure in black and white.

The Monday before, the boiler — likely already low on pressure — burned gas in the morning and then nothing all evening, despite the evening hot-water window running. It stayed dead overnight and through Tuesday's early hot-water call, until I topped it up around 07:15.

Look at Monday: 0.99 m³, but all of it in the morning. The evening hot-water window — the one I'd have assumed was fine — burned zero. Tuesday started the same way until my manual fix.

What this means If that watchdog had been live the night before my cold shower, it would have pinged me at 18:30 Monday — a full twelve hours before I put my hand on a cold tank. The failure was sitting in the data the whole time.

What I actually built

Two small pieces in Home Assistant, both free.

1 · "Any Heat Demand"

A single switch that turns on if any source is asking for heat — the tank's hot-water control, or any radiator zone on either controller. Covers the whole house.

2 · The watchdog

Starts a clock when heat is demanded. If it's still demanded after an hour and the gas meter hasn't moved past a tiny floor, it alerts. At most once per failing window.

The alert arrives on my phone and watch, straight to the Home Assistant dashboard. It's read-only by design — it reads the meter and tells me. It never sends a command to the boiler, so there's no way for it to make things worse, only to warn me sooner.

What I'd tell anyone else

1. Check what you already have before buying anything. The answer was sitting in a gas meter I wasn't even looking at.

2. Command is not proof. Trust measurements over requests. "The schedule ran" tells you nothing if nothing actually happened.

3. You don't need real-time to catch a slow death. Half-hourly was plenty. The boiler doesn't fail in milliseconds — it fails for whole windows at a time.

4. Software first. The "obvious" fix (add a temperature probe, wire it up) wasn't wrong — it was premature. The software-only answer worked for free, in minutes.

Bottom line A problem that looked like it needed hardware — and a ladder, and cold hands — was solved entirely with a smart meter the house already had and a short Home Assistant automation. Cost: £0. And it would have warned me the evening before I ever put a hand in cold water.
