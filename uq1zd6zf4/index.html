Where Do We Go From Here?

No fence-sitting. I did the research, looked at what the best historians on both sides actually concede, and here's the answer.

Sourced from UN, ICJ, ICC, OCHA and IPC data · September 2026

Everyone's got an opinion on this and most of them are borrowed. So I went and checked. What follows is what the evidence supports — including the uncomfortable bits people on both sides would rather skip.

The verdict

7 October was a war crime. So is what came after. Hamas killed 1,200 people and took 251 hostages. Israel then killed roughly sixty times as many Palestinians, most of them civilians, and the world's courts and the world's leading genocide scholars have called the campaign what it is. Meanwhile the West Bank is being annexed piece by piece — and that has nothing to do with Hamas at all.

The "both sides" crowd makes one mistake: they answer "is Hamas terrible?" (yes) and pretend that answers "is Israel's conduct lawful?" (no). Those are different questions. Only one of them is settled by the evidence, and it isn't settled in Israel's favour.

The point in one line Both things are true — Hamas is brutal and Israel's response is worse. But they aren't equal, because one was a day of atrocity by a gang, and the other is a state doing it for three years with Western backing.

The facts nobody seriously disputes

  • 7 October: ~1,200 killed in Israel, 251 hostages. The UN's own investigators called it war crimes and crimes against humanity. Even the harshest critics of Israel concede this.
  • Gaza: ~75,000 dead by August 2026 per OCHA. Here's the bit that matters: in January 2026 the Israeli military itself accepted the ~71,000 figure for those killed by direct Israeli fire. Its own intel database put ~83% of the dead as civilians (+972). Official counts are widely treated as undercounts.
  • Famine: the IPC declared famine in Gaza City in August 2025 — the first famine ever declared in the Middle East. You don't stumble into famine. It's policy.
  • Displacement: 90% of Gaza's population driven from their homes, most of them ten times or more.
  • The courts: the ICJ found genocide "plausible" 16 judges to 1, and ruled the occupation itself unlawful. The ICC has arrest warrants out for Netanyahu and Gallant for starving civilians as a weapon. Still standing.
  • The West Bank: 54 new settlements in one year — a record. 1,828 settler attacks in 2025. 93% of investigations closed with nobody charged. Amnesty and the UN both call it annexation, because it is.
  • And since the October 2025 ceasefire: another 856 Palestinians killed by May 2026.

The history card, played and checked

The justifications get trotted out every time. I checked each one against the record — including the archives dug up by Israel's own historians.

"God gave us this land 3,000 years ago"

True as scripture, worth nothing in law. No country on earth holds title on a two-thousand-year-old tenancy. Real to people, irrelevant to law.

"A land without a people"

Every census says otherwise. 1922: 78% Muslim. 1946: 67% Arab. Jewish land ownership in 1947: about 6-7%. The phrase was invented by Victorian Christians, not by any headcount. The founding line is just not true.

"Britain promised us Palestine"

Britain promised the same land to two peoples at once. Its own 1939 committee admitted the promise to the Arabs "allows no room for doubt". A promise knowingly made twice is a scam, not a title deed.

"The Arabs rejected partition — we accepted"

True, they rejected it. Now look at what was on the table: 56.5% of the land to a third of the population. You'd reject that too. The rejection happened; calling it madness is spin.

"The Palestinians left voluntarily in 1948"

This one's dead, killed by Benny Morris — the historian Israel's own side cites. The "Arab radio told them to flee" order has never been found in any archive. Deir Yassin and the expulsions at Lod and Ramle are documented fact. The Nakba happened and it was partly forced. End of.

"There was never a Palestinian nation"

No state, true. But a Palestinian identity is documented from the 1910s. Nations don't need a state first — the Jews didn't have one in 1890 either. Technically true, rhetorically worthless.

"Jews were expelled too — it was a swap"

~850,000 Jews left Arab countries, ~700,000 Palestinians were driven out. Both real. But nobody signed a swap deal, and the law doesn't treat them the same. Real grievance, false equivalence.

"1967 was self-defence; the territories are disputed, not occupied"

The strike was lawful pre-emption against a threat that wasn't imminent — even Oren, Israel's own ex-ambassador, calls it partly a war of choice. And "disputed not occupied" is a fringe view against the ICJ, the Security Council and the Red Cross. Sixty years of occupation doesn't become legal by renaming it.

What the serious people on both sides admit

This is what convinced me more than anything — where the experts on each team hand the other side its points:

  • Benny Morris — the strongest pro-Israel historian alive — concedes the 1948 expulsions, says most of Gaza's dead are civilians, calls Israel's transfer rhetoric a cheat, and has warned Israelis are being conditioned toward genocide. His own side's star witness.
  • Norman Finkelstein — the loudest pro-Palestinian voice — openly says Hamas committed war crimes on 7 October. Doesn't hide from it.
  • Omer Bartov — Israeli-born, ex-IDF, the world's top genocide scholar. He spent 2023 saying "not yet genocide". By 2025 he'd changed his mind (Guardian, NYT). When the man who writes the textbooks on genocide — and served in the IDF — says it's genocide, listen.
The crux Both sides agree Hamas hides among civilians. They split on whether that licenses the scale of destruction. The law judges proportionality against the whole campaign — not against the anger of 7 October. A 60:1 death ratio, famine and 90% displacement is a pattern, not "a hard war".

So where do we go from here?

Any future has to pass three tests: safety for Israelis, a state for Palestinians, and law. Fail one and you get the next war, guaranteed — we've seen this film every decade since 1948.

  • 1. The ceasefire has to be an exit, not a pause. It's holding, but the phases behind it are stalled. A ceasefire that stops the shooting but leaves the occupation is just resetting the clock for round three.
  • 2. Stop the West Bank spiral. Record settlements, E1, de facto annexation — no two-state future survives that. If Israel wants to annex, fine: say so, and accept what follows — one state, and a straight choice between equal citizenship or apartheid. Right now it's taking the worst of both.
  • 3. Accountability both ways or not at all. Hamas has answered for 7 October. Netanyahu and Gallant have warrants. Western governments shielding one side while condemning the other is exactly why most of the world stopped believing in the "rules-based order".
  • 4. Politics, not just aid. Hamas and Netanyahu's coalition need each other — each proves the other side has no partner. Nothing has ever moved this conflict except a political horizon: proper Palestinian leadership, Arab money for rebuilding, and American pressure applied to both.
The bottom line Israel exists and nobody serious is proposing to undo that. But founding a state out of necessity doesn't buy a permanent licence to do whatever you like afterwards. The history cards don't survive the archive. What's left is simple: two peoples, one land — and right now one side holds all the power, most of the weapons, and nearly all the legal liability. The next move sits in Jerusalem and Washington, not in the rubble of Gaza.
