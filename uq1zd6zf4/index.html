Evidence Review · Israel–Palestine

Where Do We Go From Here?

A no-fence-sitting review of the facts, the law, the history — and what a workable path forward actually looks like.

Compiled from UN, ICJ, ICC, OCHA, IPC and peer-reviewed sources · September 2026

This brief was built from parallel research lanes: one on the verified evidence base (casualties, legal rulings, humanitarian data), one on the strongest arguments from each side, and one on the historical claims. The verdicts below are where the evidence lands — including where the most credible scholars on each side concede ground to the other.

The verdict, up front

7 October 2023 was a war crime. Israel's response has been a disproportionate campaign that serious genocide scholars, Amnesty, Human Rights Watch and the ICC prosecutor describe as genocide or its component crimes — and the ongoing West Bank annexation is illegal land-grabbing regardless of anything Hamas does.

Both things are true at once, but they are not equal in weight. One was a day of atrocity by a non-state group that has been militarily dismantled. The other is a state, armed and shielded by Western governments, that has killed roughly sixty times more people, created famine, and is annexing territory while its leader dodges an arrest warrant.

Why "both sides" fails "Hamas is terrible" is true. But it is an answer to the wrong question. It is not an answer to "is Israel's conduct lawful or proportionate?" — and on that question the evidence is one-directional.

What isn't in dispute

  • 7 October 2023: ~1,200 killed in Israel, 251 hostages taken. UN Commission of Inquiry documented Hamas war crimes and crimes against humanity.
  • Gaza deaths: ~75,000 by August 2026 per OCHA (Gaza MoH figures) — and in January 2026 the Israeli military itself accepted ~71,000 killed by direct Israeli fire. An IDF intelligence database reported by +972 Magazine put ~83% of the dead as civilians. A Lancet Global Health survey and a Max Planck Institute demographic estimate (100–126k violent deaths) support treating official counts as undercounts.
  • Famine: the IPC declared famine in Gaza Governorate in August 2025 — the first Middle East famine declaration in the IPC's history.
  • Displacement: ~1.9 million people (≈90% of Gaza's population) displaced at least once, many ten or more times.
  • Legal record: the ICJ found plausibility of genocide 16-of-17 judges in provisional measures, and ruled in July 2024 that the occupation itself is unlawful. The ICC issued arrest warrants for Netanyahu and Gallant for starvation as a method of warfare — still standing.
  • West Bank: record settlement expansion (54 new settlements in 2025), 1,828 settler attacks in 2025 (highest since recording began), 93% of investigations closed with no indictment, and de facto annexation assessed by Amnesty and the UN.
  • Since the October 2025 ceasefire: broadly holding, but OCHA documented 856 further Palestinian fatalities by mid-May 2026.

The historical claims, judged

Justifications for Israel's founding and territorial rights rest on a stack of historical claims. Here is each one against the archival record:

"God gave us this land 3,000 years ago"

True as scripture, irrelevant as law. No modern state's title rests on residence two millennia ago — that standard would dismantle half the world map. Emotionally real, legally weightless.

"A land without a people for a people without a land"

False by every census. 1922: 78% Muslim, ~10% Jewish. 1946: 67% Arab. Jewish land ownership by 1947: ~6–7%. The phrase came from Victorian Christian Restorationists (Shaftesbury 1854, Zangwill 1901), not any census. The founding demographic claim is simply untrue.

"Britain promised us Palestine"

Britain triple-dealt. Its own 1939 committee concluded the McMahon–Hussein correspondence "allows no room for doubt" that Palestine was promised to the Arabs — while promising the same land to the Zionists in the Balfour Declaration (1917). A promise made knowingly to two peoples at once is an imperial convenience, not a title deed.

"Arabs rejected partition; we accepted it"

Partly fair — the Jewish Agency accepted Resolution 181; Arab leaders rejected it without a counter-offer. But the offer gave 56.5% of the land to 33% of the population, with ~45% Arabs inside the proposed Jewish state. Any people offered a minority's land to a minority population would reject it. The rejection happened; calling it irrational is dishonest.

"Palestinians left voluntarily in 1948"

Killed by the archives — by Benny Morris, the revisionist historian Israel's own side cites most. The "Arab radio order to flee" was never substantiated; the Deir Yassin massacre and the explicit expulsion of Lod and Ramle are documented. The live debate is only whether it was war contingency (Morris) or master plan (Pappé). The Nakba was real and partially forced.

"There was never a Palestinian nation"

No state ever existed — true. But a distinct Palestinian identity is documented from the 1910s (the Falastin newspaper, 1911; Muslim–Christian Associations, 1918). Nations don't require a prior state — ask the Kurds, or indeed the Jews in 1890. Technically true, rhetorically hollow.

"It was a population exchange"

~850,000 Jews left Arab states and ~700,000 Palestinians were displaced — both real. But there was no treaty, no agreed swap, and international law treats them asymmetrically. A real grievance and a false equivalence.

"1967 was defensive; the territories are disputed, not occupied"

The first strike was lawful pre-emption against a real but not imminent threat — Shlaim, Segev and even Oren converge on "pre-emptive in form, partly a war of choice." And "disputed not occupied" is a fringe position against the ICJ, UN Security Council and ICRC consensus. A half-defensible war, followed by a 58-year occupation that is indefensible in law.

Where the serious scholars concede

The cross-checks are what make the verdict robust:

  • Benny Morris (pro-Israel historian) — concedes the expulsion narrative, that the "voluntary transfer" rhetoric is a cheat, that most of Gaza's dead are civilians, that barring journalists from Gaza was foolish — and has warned that Israelis are being "conditioned" toward genocide, while arguing the legal intent threshold isn't quite met.
  • Norman Finkelstein (Palestinian side) — explicitly concedes Hamas committed war crimes on 7 October rather than denying the attack.
  • Omer Bartov — Israeli-born, IDF veteran, and the world's leading genocide historian. His initial 2023 caution makes his later verdict — that the campaign crossed the line — carry unusual weight (Guardian, NYT).
The crux Both sides agree Hamas embeds among civilians. They split on whether that licenses the scale of destruction. International law judges proportionality against the campaign as a whole — not against the anger of 7 October. A 75:1 death ratio, famine and 90% displacement is a pattern, not "a hard war."

So: where do we go from here?

Being clear-eyed about the record points somewhere specific. A workable future has to satisfy three tests: security for Israelis, self-determination for Palestinians, and legality. Anything failing one of the three fails all three eventually — every generation since 1948 has relearned this.

1. The ceasefire must become an off-ramp

The October 2025 ceasefire holds but its later phases are stalled. A ceasefire that ends the shooting without ending the occupation just resets the clock for the next war. The UN Security Council-endorsed framework only works if phased withdrawal and reconstruction are enforced, not aspirational.

2. Stop the West Bank spiral

No two-state future survives record settlement expansion, E1, and de facto annexation. If the goal is permanent annexation, say so honestly and accept the consequences: one state, and the choice between equal citizenship or apartheid. The current path is the worst of both worlds.

3. Accountability cuts both ways

Hamas committed crimes on 7 October; Israeli leaders face ICC warrants. Selective outrage — prosecuting one side's crimes and shielding the other's — is what discredited the "rules-based order" in most of the world's eyes. Accountability either applies or it means nothing.

4. Restart politics, not just aid

Hamas and the Netanyahu coalition are mirror images: each uses the other to prove there is no partner. A credible political horizon — reformed Palestinian leadership, Arab-state reconstruction guarantees, US pressure on both — is the only thing that has ever moved this conflict.

The honest framing: Israel's existence today is a settled fact nobody serious proposes undoing — but a state founded out of necessity does not get a permanent licence. The historical trump cards mostly don't survive contact with the archive. What remains is simply: two peoples, one land, one side holding all the power and most of the legal liability — and power plus obligation means the burden of the next step sits mainly in Jerusalem and Washington, not in the rubble of Gaza.
